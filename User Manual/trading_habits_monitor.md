# How the dashboard reads your trading habits

*A short explainer — no technical background needed.*

## The one-line idea
The dashboard doesn't just tell you whether a trading habit made or lost money this week. It tells you
whether you're actually **getting better at the habit over time** — and it does that by measuring two
different things, because one number on its own can fool you.

## First, a quick reminder: what's a "detector"
The journal watches for specific behaviors in your trades — things like *trading impulsively*, *taking
trades with no real setup*, or *revenge-trading after a loss*. Each of these is a "detector." It just
flags, week by week, "this behavior showed up, and here's what it did." Think of each detector as a little
sensor for one habit.

## Why we don't judge a habit by profit/loss alone

The obvious way to track a habit is by the money it costs you. But dollars lie about behavior, for two
reasons:

- **Luck.** You can trade impulsively and get lucky — a sloppy trade that happens to win. Judged by
  dollars, that looks "fine," even though the *behavior* was bad. Good outcomes hide bad habits.
- **Size.** A $500 loss when you're trading big isn't the same habit-problem as a $500 loss when you're
  trading small. Dollars get distorted by how much you happened to bet.

So a habit can look like it's "improving" purely because you traded smaller or got lucky that week — not
because you actually did the bad thing less.

**The fix: lead with frequency, not dollars.** Frequency = *how often you did the behavior* (for example,
"2 of my 10 trades this week were impulsive" = 20%). Whether a trade won or lost is partly luck; whether
you *chose to take it* is pure behavior, fully in your control. So "impulsive trades went from 30% of my
trades to 10%" is an honest sign you're changing — in a way dollars can never be.

Dollars still matter, so they're shown too — just as the *secondary* number. Frequency answers "am I doing
it less," dollars answer "what is it costing me." Different questions.

## Why one trend line isn't enough: the two-trend ("two-slope") idea

Here's the part most tools miss. To know if a habit is really improving, you have to watch **two trends at
once**, because they can move in opposite directions and disagree:

1. **How often** you do it (frequency).
2. **How badly it hurts when you do** (the average damage per occurrence — call it severity).

Picture this: over a few weeks you cut your impulsive trades way down — great, frequency is dropping. But
the few impulsive trades you *still* take have gotten huge and reckless — severity is rising. Add it up in
dollars and it might look flat, like "no change." But that's not no change — it's a completely different
problem. You're doing it less, but the ones that slip through are now the dangerous ones.

Only by tracking **both** trends can the dashboard catch that. One number would have told you "meh, about
the same." Two numbers tell you the truth.

## What you'll actually see: the verdict

Instead of making you stare at a chart and guess, the dashboard reads those two trends and just **tells you
in a sentence** which way the habit is going:

- Doing it **less** and **cheaper** when you do → *"Improving."*
- Doing it **less** but **costlier each time** → *"You're doing it less, but each one hurts more."*
- Doing it **more** and **costlier** → *"Getting worse."*
- About the **same** → *"Holding steady."*

It also catches a **relapse** — if a habit's been improving for weeks but you backslid in the most recent
week, it'll flag that, so a good streak doesn't hide a recent slip.

(For *good* habits — like patiently waiting for trades to come to you — the logic flips: doing it *more* is
the win, and the dashboard tells you if that good habit is **building** or **slipping**.)

## A quick example

Say the dashboard looks at your impulsive trades over the last couple of months:

- **How often:** roughly the same, week after week — you haven't actually cut them down.
- **How badly:** the average damage per impulsive trade has climbed from about $140 to about $600.

A dollars-only view would've shown a jumpy, random-looking line and told you nothing useful. The two-trend
view says, plainly: *"You're trading impulsively just as often as before — but each one is now costing you
four times as much. This isn't shrinking, it's getting heavier."* That's something you can act on.

## One thing that keeps it honest

The dashboard only counts weeks where you actually traded enough to have a real sample. If you barely
traded one week, that week isn't treated as "I beat the habit!" — it's just skipped. So a quiet week or a
vacation can't fake improvement. The verdict always reflects weeks where you genuinely had the chance to
do the behavior.

## The payoff

You stop guessing whether you're improving, and you stop being fooled by lucky weeks or big weeks. You get
a plain, honest read on each habit — *am I doing this less, and is it hurting less* — so the time you spend
reviewing turns into actual change, not just a record of what happened.
